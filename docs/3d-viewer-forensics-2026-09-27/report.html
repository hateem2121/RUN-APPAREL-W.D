Garment Viewer Forensics

Why some RUN APPAREL garments lose parts when zoomed out, flicker white, show "blend" prints as solid, and look lower quality than CLO. Every claim below was checked in the code, in the live files, or by rendering the live site.

16 live garments inspected14 shrink reports readAll 16 rendered live: front, side and back, desktop and phone0 files changed

The short version

1Parts vanishing when zoomed out

The anti-flicker "nudge" is too strong from far away. On Minecut it pushes the marble print over the "EXTRA" waistband, from every angle, in every colour and on phones too. On the soccer shirt it pushes the pink print over the collar. The other 14 garments are not affected this way.

2White flicker

The old fixes work. Four garments still blink, each for a different reason: the soccer shirt's knit texture, the X-Milo Pro Bib's cut-out print edges, the Armor-Tech Jacket's stitching, and the vest's stitching plus cut-out edges.

3"Blend" prints look solid

CLO writes almost every print at 100% strength when it exports. The 3D file format also has no "blend" mode. So the effect is lost before your robot sees the file.

4Low quality

File size is not the limit (files are 1.8–7.8 MB against a 40 MB ceiling). The real limits are the 4096-pixel print cap and phone graphics memory. Raising every limit would not help and could crash iPhones.

How a garment travels

The designer saves a big 3D file from CLO. A robot shrinks it so it loads on a phone, turning fancy materials into simple ones. It checks a few safety rules, then files it in the showroom. When a buyer scans the tag, the viewer shows it, and two helpers try to stop printed layers fighting with the cloth. The coloured boxes show where each problem enters.

1

Parts invisible when zoomed out

Minecut waistband, Classic Soccer Shirt collar✓ Verified · 95%
In kid words

A printed layer and the cloth under it sit almost on top of each other, so the computer can't tell which is in front. The fix gives the print a little push toward you. But the push is measured in screen dots. When you zoom out, one dot covers more of the garment, so the push gets bigger. On Minecut it gets big enough to shove the marble print in front of the waistband that really sits on top of it. The waistband disappears.

Confidence95%
  • ✓Switching off only the marble print's nudge gives exactly the same picture as switching off every nudge. That one nudge is the cause.
  • ✓Against a sharp reference with the nudge off, the live zoomed-out view is wrong on 6.28% of the garment, and 91% of those wrong dots are too pale. With the nudge off it drops to 3.55%, and the rest is normal edge smoothing plus speckles on the skirt edges.
  • ✓The nudge has two parts. The "per screen dot" part (factor −8) does all the work; the fixed part (units −8) changes nothing measurable. The per-dot part is the one that grows as you zoom out: at full zoom-out each screen dot covers about 3.4 mm of garment, against about 1.8 mm at the default view.
  • ✓Same defect on the Classic Soccer Shirt: the pink body print breaks through the blue collar. The nudge changes 0.68% of the garment at the default view and 1.27% zoomed out.
  • ✓Minecut is affected from every angle and in every colour: the nudge changes 2.8–6.5% of the garment from the front, side and back, and Blush gives the same numbers as Sage.
  • ✓It happens on phones too. On an iPhone-sized screen the Minecut waistband is cut down at the default view and shrinks to a small patch when zoomed out (1.3–3.2% of the garment changes).
  • ✓All 16 garments were tested. Only Minecut and the Classic Soccer Shirt carry a nudge on a big solid print, and only they lose parts. On Geovent Tennis Dress the nudge helps: switch it off and the camouflage print breaks into holes. On the other 13 the nudge changes under 1% and nothing visibly goes missing.
  • ✓Why the repo's tests missed it: the strength −8 was tuned "from a fixed camera" (decal-depth-bias.ts:125), the safety check was run on a garment with no nudged solid print, and the browser test checks that the nudge is applied, not how the garment looks when zoomed out.
  • ✓The nudge is still needed. Without it, speckles return along Minecut's skirt edges. The fix has to shrink the push, not delete it.
  • ◎207 print-and-colour pairs across 12 garments have ink almost the same colour as the cloth (the robot's "ink vs cloth" check). Those prints fade into the cloth at any zoom, and first when far away. Worst: Classic Soccer Shirt 75 of 110, Endurance Tracksuit 38 of 155, The Aggressor Jersey 33 of 135.
  • ✕Frustum culling (hiding parts "off screen"): model-viewer switches it off for every part.
  • ✕The far plane (the back wall of the view) is always at least twice the camera distance, so it never cuts the garment.
  • ✕Level-of-detail swapping: none exists in the repo or in model-viewer.
  • ✕Depth precision: the near-plane fix is live (1.89 m zoomed out), which separates layers about 0.0005 mm apart. Minecut's tightest gap is 0.027 mm.
  • ✕Smaller picture copies (mipmaps), my first main suspect: turning them off only moved the error from 6.28% to 6.08%, a small side effect rather than the cause. Alpha-to-coverage made it worse (6.57%).
Evidence (files and lines)

The strength, apps/viewer/src/lib/decal-depth-bias.ts:145:

export const OFFSET_FACTOR = -8
export const OFFSET_UNITS = -8

Where the pipeline's note sets it on a solid layer, decal-depth-bias.ts:260-262:

backing.polygonOffset = true
backing.polygonOffsetFactor = overlay ? overlay.factor : OFFSET_FACTOR

Culling off, @google/model-viewer 4.3.1 ModelViewerGLTFInstance.js:42: node.frustumCulled = false; · Far plane, features/controls.js:572: const far = Math.abs(2 * maximumRadius);

The live file minecut-motion-2026-09-03-optimized.glb stores extras.depthBias = { factor: -8, units: -8 } on 5 "Material_Graphic" materials (one per colourway).

The proof test (done)

All 16 live garments; front, side and back; default and full zoom-out; nudge on and off. Minecut also in a second colour and on a phone-sized screen, compared with a 4× sharper reference with the nudge off.

Fix direction

Make the push not grow with zoom (cap it or scale it by camera distance), or stop flagging a big print panel that has another layer on top of it. Then re-run the flicker test so the skirt edges stay clean.

2

White flicker is back

Measured on all 16 garments; four still blink, each for its own reason✓ Verified · 85%
In kid words

I turned each garment a tiny bit at a time and counted dots that blink: change, then change back. Plain movement doesn't do that, so it isolates flicker. Then I hid one part at a time to see which part the blinking lives on. It's like finding which bulb in a string of lights is flickering by unplugging them one by one.

Confidence85%
  • ✓The old fixes are live and working. The near plane reads 0.85 m at the default view and 1.89 m zoomed out. On Minecut the nudge cuts blinking from 0.27% to 0.02% of the garment.
  • ✓Classic Soccer Shirt (0.64%): the bumpy knit texture. Switching the weave map off drops it to 0.03%. three.js calms sparkle using only the shape's own surface directions, never the bump texture (dFdx( nonPerturbedNormal )).
  • ✓X-Milo Pro Bib (0.94% front, 1.16% back, the highest): its cut-out side-panel print ("Material_Graphic"). Hiding it drops blinking to 0.004%. Switching on alpha-to-coverage (edge smoothing for cut-outs) drops it to 0.09%, which is 91% less.
  • ✓Armor-Tech Jacket (0.54%): its topstitch thread, stored as thin see-through strips. Hiding it drops blinking to 0.01%.
  • ✓Women Zip-Up Vest (0.27%): no single part. Hiding the topstitch halves it (0.13%), and alpha-to-coverage cuts it by 40% (0.16%). Hiding the zip parts changes nothing, so the blinking "along the zip" is the stitching beside it.
  • ✓The other 12 garments blink on under 0.2% of the garment, which is hard to notice.
  • ✕Making surfaces less shiny changed nothing on the Bib, the Jacket or the Vest, so glossiness isn't the cause there.
  • ✓Risk to watch: on 10 of 14 garments a print sits closer to its cloth than two steps of the storage grid. The nudge is all that separates them, and 66 layers were "held for review" with no nudge (27 on Endurance Tracksuit).
  • ✕My first report blamed "151 see-through prints with no nudge". Correction: only about 12 of those 151 are prints. The rest are fabric, thread, tape and labels.
Evidence (files and lines)

three 0.183.2 lights_physical_fragment.glsl.js:7

vec3 dxy = max( abs( dFdx( nonPerturbedNormal ) ), abs( dFdy( nonPerturbedNormal ) ) );

Near plane: apps/viewer/src/lib/camera-near-plane.ts:116-121. Held-for-review rule: tools/asset-pipeline/src/overlay-annotate.ts:225 with OVERLAY_AUTO_CONFIDENCE = 0.7 at overlay-depth.ts:79. Grid warning: tools/asset-pipeline/src/precision.ts:61.

The proof test (done)

All 16 garments, front and back, turned in half-degree steps. The four worst were repeated with weave maps off, with alpha-to-coverage on, with shine reduced, and with each part hidden in turn.

Fix direction

Turn on alpha-to-coverage for cut-out prints (the fix for the Bib), calm bump-texture glitter in the viewer (for the Soccer Shirt), and give topstitch thread a steadier material (for the Jacket and the Vest).

3

CLO "blend" prints render solid

Mostly lost inside CLO's export, not in your robot✓ Verified · 75%
In kid words

The 3D file type your viewer uses knows only three kinds of sticker: solid, cut-out (like a stencil) and see-through glass. It has no "mix with the fabric" sticker. On top of that, CLO saves almost every print as fully solid when it exports. By the time the file reaches the robot, the blend is already gone.

Confidence75%
  • ✓The robot records what CLO wrote before touching anything. In all 14 reports, almost every listed print arrived at "opacity 100%". Only The Aggressor Jersey and Geovent Tennis Dress show 5 faint prints each.
  • ✓The glTF rulebook has exactly three modes. Its see-through mode is "the normal painting operation (i.e. the Porter and Duff over operator)". There is no multiply or blend mode.
  • ✓How the robot sorted CLO's see-through materials across 14 garments: 180 made solid, 440 made into cut-outs, 188 kept see-through.
  • ✓Faint prints that CLO did export are kept faint: "Skull" at 13% on The Aggressor Uniform and The Aggressor Jersey Men, "Asset 2" at 16% on Geovent Tennis Dress.
  • ◎140 of 447 print materials carry no fabric bump (weave) map. Every print on X-Milo Pro Skin-Suit, Classic Soccer Shirt, Aero-Tech Windbreaker and Apex Flex Pullover lacks one. With no weave on the print layer, fabric can't show through it.
  • ✓The repo already says it: "CLO does not export a graphic's opacity map."
Evidence (files and lines)

The sorting rule, tools/asset-pipeline/src/textures.ts:457-470:

if (factor < OPAQUE_FACTOR_THRESHOLD) return 'keep'
if (isCutoutProfile(alpha)) return 'MASK'
...
return 'OPAQUE'

tools/asset-pipeline/src/raw-census.ts:360: "A faint print must be baked into the PNG — CLO does not export a graphic's opacity map." Khronos material.schema.json, alphaMode BLEND.

The one test that settles it

Pick one "blend" print. Export it from CLO and read what the export file says about that print (strength, see-through pixels, bump map) before the robot touches it.

Fix direction

In CLO, bake the blended look into the print picture itself, and give the print the fabric's bump map. That is the only form a glTF file can carry.

4

Some models look low quality

Your idea: "increase all the MB sizes"✓ Verified · 70%
In kid words

The suitcase isn't full. Every garment file is small (1.8–7.8 MB) and the limit is 40 MB. What squeezes quality is a size cap on each print picture (4096 dots on the long side) and the phone's graphics memory, which iPhones run out of at about 256 MB. Making everything bigger would mostly fill that phone memory, not make garments sharper.

Confidence70%
  • ✓All 14 reports stored "Balanced". Five were bumped to Highest quality automatically because their raw file was under 50 MB. The mesh trimmer ran on only 2 of 14, so the Detail setting changes almost nothing for these garments.
  • ✓A print was shrunk to fit on 9 of 14 garments ("fine lettering on them is softer"). Example: Minecut's slogan is 5148 × 310 and was shrunk to 4096 wide.
  • ✓Phone graphics memory already reaches 224 MB (The Aggressor Uniform), 201 MB (The Aggressor Jersey) and 192 MB (The Aggressor Jersey Men). A square 8192 print would add about 358 MB on its own.
  • ✓Correction to my first report: surface directions (normals) are not squeezed at this setting; they keep full precision. Positions use a 14-bit grid and texture coordinates 16-bit.
  • ✓The "fold flat maps" step removed maps on only 2 garments (3 maps), so it isn't a widespread cause.
  • ✓No safety gate looks at the picture. specGate.ts only asks "is this a valid glTF file?"; the other refusals are about structure. Shrunk prints, low ink contrast and phone memory are warnings only.
  • ◎Weave maps are mostly 1024 px, below the 2048 cap, so that is how CLO exported them. Close-ups of fabric will look soft.
  • ?Prints saved at gloss 0.20 may look plasticky under the studio light.
Evidence (files and lines)

Limits: packages/shared/src/media.ts:12 SIZE_WARNING_BYTES = 8 MB, :20 GLB_HARD_MAX_BYTES = 40 MB. Presets: packages/shared/src/shrink.ts:220-275. Auto-upgrade: shrink.ts:192. Phone budget: tools/asset-pipeline/src/texture-fold.ts PHONE_GPU_BUDGET_BYTES = 256 MB. Gates: apps/shrink/src/index.ts:459-532.

glTF-Transform 4.5.0 meshopt() at level "high" leaves NORMAL out of its quantize pattern: /^(POSITION|TEXCOORD|JOINTS|WEIGHTS|COLOR)(_\d+)?$/.

My honest answer on "increase the MB"

Don't raise everything. Raise the print cap only for long, thin prints (Minecut's slogan at full size costs about 8.5 MB of phone memory), and let phone memory decide the cap instead of picture width. Export weave maps at 2048 from CLO for sharper close-ups.

5

Other issues

Transparency sorting, lighting, missing records◎ Mixed
  • ✓No order-independent transparency (a way to draw see-through layers in the right order) exists in three.js 0.183.2 or model-viewer 4.3.1. See-through parts are sorted as whole pieces, so two of them can swap order as a garment turns.
  • ◎Some fabrics are kept see-through: T3456772LS2 on Women Zip-Up Vest, and T2359355B-TDFX8-D1 on The Aggressor Uniform and The Aggressor Jersey Men. If they are not sheer, the inside of the garment can show through.
  • ✓Apex Flex Pullover and Capsule Core Hoodie have no shrink report in the database. They were processed outside the robot, so their settings can't be audited.
  • ✕Armor-Tech Jacket zipper teeth at 0% strength: an invisible zipper by design (you confirmed). Not a bug.
  • ✓Studio lighting is sensible: soft studio light map, neutral colour handling, exposure 1, soft shadow (Stage.tsx:962-966).
