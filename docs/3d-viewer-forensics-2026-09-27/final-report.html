Why garments look wrong in the 3D viewer

The complete record of a read-only investigation into RUN APPAREL's 16 live garments on 27 September 2026. A cloud session worked from the code, the live files and the live site. A second session on the owner's Mac added the raw CLO exports, the CLO renders and a real iPhone simulator. Both are merged here. Nothing in the code, the database or the live site was changed.

The short version

1

Parts vanish because the anti-flicker nudge over-reaches. On Minecut Motion it pushes the marble print over the "EXTRA" waistband; on the Classic Soccer Shirt it pushes the pink print through the collar. newThis happens at the normal starting view too, on every screen and in real iOS Safari, not only when zoomed out. The root cause: the pipeline only looks 2 mm above a print for anything covering it, and those layers sit 2.5–7 mm above.

2

Four garments still flicker, each for its own reason: the X-Milo Pro Bib's cut-out side-panel print, the Soccer Shirt's knit bump texture, the Armor-Tech Jacket's see-through stitching, and the Women Zip-Up Vest's stitching plus cut-out edges. The old fixes (near plane, nudge) are working.

3

Soft "blend" prints are lost inside CLO's export, before the robot sees the file. newTraced end to end for three prints: the raw CLO file already renders Geovent's pattern and Minecut's marble bold, and Arisan's brush strokes at about 6% strength. The pipeline keeps each one exactly as exported.

4

File size is not what limits quality (1.8–7.8 MB against a 40 MB ceiling). The real limits are the per-picture size cap and phone graphics memory. newTwo pipeline losses were found: thin stitch lines are cut away on 12 of 15 garments with thread, and the Bib's halftone was shrunk to 8.5% of its pixels.

5

newShine and colour differences come from CLO's export values, not the pipeline. The pipeline made the Armor-Tech leather slightly less shiny.

6

newA fix for the waistband and collar works in testing: a nudge with no "per screen dot" part restored both completely (0.0–0.1% left) with no extra flicker. Not built; it needs a phone check first.

7

newApex Flex Pullover and Capsule Core Hoodie were processed by the normal robot. Their reports exist; only the links to their files are broken. The cloud session's "processed outside the robot" is corrected.

Findings

Each finding opens to show what happens in plain words, every claim with its label, the evidence, the one test that settles it, and the fix options.

1Parts disappear: the nudge over-reachesMinecut waistband, Soccer Shirt collarProven · 95%
In plain wordsA print sits a hair above the cloth, so the computer cannot always tell which is in front. The fix gives the print a small push toward you, measured in screen dots. Where the cloth curves away from you each dot covers several millimetres, so the push can be centimetres. The pipeline picks which prints get the push by checking for anything lying on top of them, but it only looks 2 mm up. Minecut's waistband sits 2.5 mm up, so the check missed it, the marble got the push, and it jumped in front of the waistband.
  • ✓Switching off only the marble print's nudge gives exactly the same picture as switching off every nudge. That one nudge is the cause.
  • ✓newIt happens at the normal starting view, at 1×, 2× and 3× screen sharpness and in real iOS Safari, not only when zoomed out. Zooming out makes it worse.
  • ✓newRoot cause: the overlay scan casts its "is anything on top of me?" ray only 2 mm (SEARCH_MM, overlay-depth.ts:226). On Minecut the white waistband covers 15.7% of the marble panel and an outer skirt layer covers 43.6%, both about 2.5 mm up. The scan saw almost none of it and scored the panel 90% "on top".
  • ✓newOn the Soccer Shirt, the fold-over collar sits 2.9–7 mm above the front print (median 5.3 mm) and covers 4.2% of it. Hiding that one piece removes the whole collar, so it is the collar.
  • ✓newThe code's safety argument (overlay-depth.ts:121-126) assumes anything on top of a nudged print is nudged too. That is only true within 0.3 mm; a layer 2.5 mm up is never nudged, so it loses.
  • ✓newProof by elimination: the raw CLO file has no nudge note. Rendered the same way, it shows the waistband and a clean collar; the shipped file loses both.
  • ✓The nudge has two parts. The "per screen dot" part (factor −8) does all the work; the fixed part (units −8) changes nothing measurable. At full zoom-out each screen dot covers about 3.4 mm of garment, against about 1.8 mm at the default view.
  • ✓Against a sharp reference with the nudge off, the zoomed-out Minecut is wrong on 6.28% of the garment, 91% of it too pale. With the nudge off it drops to 3.55%, the rest being edge smoothing and skirt-edge speckle.
  • ✓Minecut is affected from the front, side and back (2.8–6.5% of the garment changes) and in every colour. The Soccer Shirt changes 0.68% at the default view and 1.27% zoomed out.
  • ✓All 16 garments were tested. Only Minecut and the Soccer Shirt carry a nudge on a big solid print. On Geovent the nudge helps (without it the see-through camouflage print breaks into holes). On the other 13 it changes under 1% and nothing visibly goes missing.
  • ✓new"Held for review" does not mean "left alone": every held print is a cut-out, and the viewer nudges all cut-outs anyway (decal-depth-bias.ts:252). Minecut held 3 prints (confidence 0.00–0.07, their gap sits exactly on the 0.3 mm edge), the Soccer Shirt 8 (0.66–0.68).
  • ✓Why the tests missed it: the strength −8 was tuned "from a fixed camera" (decal-depth-bias.ts:125), the safety check used a garment with no nudged solid print, and the browser test checks that the nudge is applied, not how the garment looks.
  • ✕Ruled out: parts hidden as "off screen" (model-viewer turns that off), the far plane (always at least twice the camera distance), level-of-detail swaps (none exist), depth precision (the near-plane fix is live), smaller picture copies (turning them off moved the error only from 6.28% to 6.08%).
The test that settled itRender the raw file (no nudge note) and the shipped file the same way: only the shipped one loses the waistband and collar.
Fix optionsSee Fix options: a nudge with no per-dot part (tested best), a weaker per-dot part, or a scan that looks further up (tested, leaves a little behind).
2Flicker: four garments, four causesMeasured on all 16; the rest blink under 0.2%Proven · 85%
In plain wordsTurn a garment a tiny bit at a time and count dots that change and change back. Plain movement does not do that, so it isolates flicker. Then hide one part at a time to find which part the blinking lives on, like finding the faulty bulb in a string of lights.
  • ✓The old fixes are live and working: the near plane reads 0.85 m at the default view and 1.89 m zoomed out, and on Minecut the nudge cuts blinking from 0.27% to 0.02% (cloud setup).
  • ✓X-Milo Pro Bib, the worst: its cut-out side-panel print. Hiding it drops blinking to 0.004%. newWhy that print flickers: it was shrunk from 4952×7014 to 1446×2048 pixels and then cut out at half strength, so each blurred dot is chopped differently every frame. Seen in real iOS frames too.
  • ✓Classic Soccer Shirt: the knit bump texture. Switching it off drops blinking by 95% (cloud) and 96% (Mac). three.js calms sparkle using only the shape's own surface, never the bump texture.
  • ✓Armor-Tech Jacket: its stitching, stored as thin see-through strips. Hiding it drops blinking to 0.01%.
  • ✓Women Zip-Up Vest: no single part. The stitching and cut-out edges share it; the zip parts themselves change nothing.
  • ◎newThe numbers depend on the device. The cloud measured with software graphics at 1× sharpness; the Mac used its real graphics card at 2×. The Bib reads 0.94% vs 2.23%, the Jacket 0.54% vs 0.09%, and alpha-to-coverage cut the Bib's blinking 91% vs 15%. Both are shown in the chart below. Phones decide.
  • ✓Risk to watch: on 10 of 14 garments a print sits closer to its cloth than two steps of the file's storage grid. newCapsule Core Hoodie's closest print is 0.009 mm up (0.2 steps). The nudge is all that keeps these apart.
  • ✕Making surfaces less shiny changed nothing on the Bib, the Jacket or the Vest.
  • ✕An early count of "151 see-through prints with no nudge" was wrong: only about 12 of the 151 are prints; the rest are fabric, thread, tape and labels.
The test that settles the fixesRepeat the blink test for each fix on the iOS simulator and one real phone, since the two desktop setups disagree.
3"Blend" prints look wrongLost inside CLO's export, not in the robotProven · 85%
In plain wordsThe 3D file type the viewer uses knows three kinds of sticker: solid, cut-out (a stencil) and see-through glass. It has no "mix with the fabric" sticker. When CLO saves the file it also changes how some prints look. By the time the robot sees the file, the soft look is already gone, and the robot keeps what it was given.
  • ✓In all 16 shrink reports almost every print arrived at "opacity 100%". Only The Aggressor Jersey and Geovent list faint prints; Arisan's brush print and the Aggressor "Skull" are also faint.
  • ✓glTF has exactly three modes, and its see-through mode is the normal "paint over" operation. There is no multiply or blend mode.
  • ✓newGeovent pattern (Asset 2, 16%): kept exactly; the live material reads the same colour and 16% opacity; hiding it removes the pattern. The raw CLO file already renders wide bold bands where CLO's own render shows faint vertical chevrons.
  • ✓newMinecut marble: solid in the raw file and the shipped one; both render bold where CLO's render is soft.
  • ◎newArisan brush strokes (20% and 5%): kept exactly, but the picture itself is soft, so the result is about 6% strength, nearly invisible. 85%: the 1.83 GB raw file was not rendered.
  • ✓newThe Aggressor Jersey's 19% overlay survives at about CLO's strength up close.
  • ✓How the robot sorted CLO's see-through materials across 14 garments: 180 made solid, 440 cut-outs, 188 kept see-through. Faint prints stay faint ("Skull" 13%, "Asset 2" 16%).
  • ◎140 of 447 print materials carry no fabric bump map, so fabric cannot show through them (every print on the Skin-Suit, Soccer Shirt, Windbreaker and Apex).
  • ✕newDouble-sided drawing of the Geovent print was ruled out (it changes the darkening only from −21.1 to −20.7 out of 255).
Fix optionsOnly in CLO: bake the soft look into the print picture itself before export, and give the print the fabric's bump map. Untested.
4Lower quality than CLOFile size is not the limit; three real losses foundProven · 80%
In plain wordsThe suitcase is not full: every file is 1.8–7.8 MB against a 40 MB limit. What squeezes quality is a size cap on each print picture and the phone's graphics memory, which iPhones run out of at about 256 MB. Making everything bigger would mostly fill phone memory. Two specific losses do matter: thin stitching cut away, and one print shrunk far too much.
  • ✓newStitching cut away: 12 of 15 garments with thread ship it as a cut-out. From a normal distance a one-dot stitch is mostly background, so it is cut. Drawing it soft brings back 2.88% of the Bib, 2.54% of Geovent, 2.51% of the Uniform, and 0.4–0.8% on five more. On Geovent the hexagon outline and princess seams return.
  • ✓newBib halftone: 4952×7014 in the raw file, 1446×2048 shipped (8.5% of the pixels). Soft pixels rise from 8.3% to 45.3%. The pipeline's own notes record this trap and name --max-texture 4096 as the lever.
  • ✓All 16 reports stored "Balanced"; five were raised to Highest automatically because the raw file was under 50 MB. The mesh trimmer ran on only 2 of 14.
  • ✓A print was shrunk to the 4096 cap on 9 of 14 garments. Example: Minecut's slogan, 5148×310 to 4096 wide.
  • ✓Phone graphics memory already reaches 224 MB (Uniform), 201 MB and 192 MB (the two jerseys). One square 8192 print would add about 358 MB.
  • ✓Surface directions (normals) keep full precision; positions use a 14-bit grid and texture coordinates 16-bit.
  • ✓No safety gate looks at the picture: the spec gate only asks "is this a valid file?". Shrunk prints, low ink contrast and phone memory are warnings.
  • ✓newNo artwork was swapped or lost: a print that looked missing had been merged with an identical twin.
  • ◎Bump maps are mostly 1024 px as CLO exported them, so fabric close-ups look soft.
The test that settles the BibRe-run the Bib from its raw export at --max-texture 4096 and look at the side panel in close-up (4–7°).
5Shine, colour and low-contrast printsFrom CLO's export valuesStrong evidence · 75%
  • ✓newArmor-Tech patent-leather shine: CLO writes the leather at roughness 0.40 with no roughness map; the pipeline raised it to 0.50, slightly less shiny. The shine is not the pipeline's.
  • ◎newSkin-Suit darker and glossier than CLO: fabric settings are identical raw vs shipped, so the difference is CLO's renderer vs the viewer's lighting.
  • ✓newBib dots near-black: the raw file already sets the ink to near-black red.
  • ✓newSoccer Shirt contour lines: CLO's render shows fine contour lines; the raw file already shows heavy cable-knit bumps instead.
  • ◎Ink reads as bare cloth in 230 print-and-colour pairs across 14 garments (the robot's contrast check). Worst: Soccer Shirt 75 of 110, Endurance Tracksuit 38 of 155, Aggressor Jersey 33 of 135, newApex 10 of 15, Capsule 13 of 30. These may be an intended tone-on-tone design.
  • ?Prints saved at gloss 0.20 may look plasticky under the studio light (Apex's chest logo is an example).
  • ✓The studio lighting itself is sensible: soft light map, neutral colour, exposure 1, soft shadow.

  • ✓newApex: report #2 exists, but its link to a result file is empty. The live file came from an earlier run of the same upload (04:36 on 4 Sep); the report describes a re-run that finished at 17:53 and was never attached. The live file matches the report's counts (10 nudge notes, 27 cut-outs).

  • ◎Some fabrics are kept see-through on the Women Zip-Up Vest and on two Aggressor garments. If they are not really sheer, the inside of the garment can show through.
  • ✕The Armor-Tech Jacket's zipper teeth at 0% strength are an invisible zipper by design. Not a bug.

Mistakes caught along the way

  • The cloud's first reference picture still had the nudge on and hid the waistband too (spotted by the owner); it was rebuilt.
  • In the cloud's full run three garments showed 28–41% changes because the camera had not settled; the settled numbers (0.01–0.10%) were used.
  • The Mac session's first idea for Geovent (the print drawn twice because it is double-sided) was tested and ruled out.
  • A "pattern strength" number for Geovent was abandoned because CLO's studio shadows on the pleats swamped it; close-ups were compared by eye instead.
  • A print that looked "missing" in a size table had been merged with an identical twin; no artwork was lost.
  • A blink number from the iPhone simulator was not used: a real finger drag turns the garment too far between frames for the metric to work.
